## Product and cumulative changes

Full is a high-performance C17 library for dynamic ordering, stable sorting, batch ordered processing and historical ordering. Use Explicit Order for relative edits and precedence, ManagedOrder for comparator-maintained order and bounds, `lks_sort` for stable pointer-array sorting, Group/Batch for immutable sorted chunks and merge, and Snapshot for independently owned history and association bytes. These families can be used independently.

The accepted implementation combines affected-slot updates, bounded block labels with AVL fallback, and fixed-width snapshot wire conversion. The 4.1 compact Resident and Snapshot storage remains. The technical lineage is [Preview.1](V4_2_0_PREVIEW1.md), [Preview.1.2](V4_2_0_PREVIEW1_2.md), [diagnostic ceiling study](V4_2_0_PREVIEW1_2_1.md), [Preview.2](V4_2_0_PREVIEW2.md), [Preview.3](V4_2_0_PREVIEW3.md), and [RC.1](V4_2_0_RC1.md). Rejected strategies and adverse results remain in their original records.

## Measured value and tradeoffs

The [complete evidence](FULL_RC1_EVIDENCE.md) reuses the unchanged measured implementation, not a fresh Stable campaign. Matched Full/4.1 elapsed ratios are 0.629106 Windows and 0.640047 Linux overall; compare-heavy ratios are 0.197718 and 0.219173, with 45/45 wins per host. Full/native tag-range OM overall ratios are 1.052438 and 1.020154: several update-heavy conditions favor OM. The matched lifecycle process-wall ratios are 0.580369/0.526053 versus the tested Treap with incremental restore, and 1.137284/1.136323 versus endpoint flat history. Bulk Treap restore is unmeasured.

The separate selected wire-decode study reports median reductions of 32.9%/50.6%, with unchanged requested memory and wire bytes. These medians are not multiplied across campaigns. Native OrdWeave generated replay supports compatibility, with approximately 1.002/0.997 operation-latency ratios against Preview.1.2; application publication and WAL dominate. Its official dependency remains v4.1.0. The measurements are neither field adoption nor a database-wide speedup claim.

Resident allocation, block slack, comparator/callback cost, O(N+A) capture/export and retained history remain resource costs. Application payloads, identities, transactions, callback effects and external synchronization remain application-owned. The [selection guide](FULL_SELECTION_GUIDE.md) retains counterexamples and unmeasured alternatives.
