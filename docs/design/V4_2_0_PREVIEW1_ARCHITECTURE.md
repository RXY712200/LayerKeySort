# Preview.1 live-order architecture decision

Stable derives two absolute block ranks from AVL subtree counts. The candidate keeps the threaded blocks, stable residents and AVL. Cross-block comparison advances the lower-height side until the blocks are siblings or one is the immediate ancestor of the other. Height strictly increases upward, which prevents a walk from passing the common ancestor before the other reaches it. Child direction supplies precedence. Same-block comparison uses local indices. Subtree counts remain maintained and checked by diagnostics. Managed searches and bulk restoration preserve their existing topology and contracts.

Insertion sets membership on the incoming resident and updates local positions from the insertion point onward. Ordinary removal clears its old tail slot and updates the shifted suffix. Cross-block movement updates its destination and, when no repair is needed, the shifted source suffix. Split, merge and redistribution retain full location repair because they change membership. These operations keep fallible allocations before publication. No record, block or order size changes.

A research-only dynamic 64-bit block midpoint prototype retained AVL fallback. Endpoint allocation used a fixed stride and internal splits assigned midpoint tags. Repeated insertion before an interior anchor exhausted a gap at insertion 1,057. The prototype disabled labels for that live order and preserved tested comparisons through 10,000 insertions. It avoids global relabel writes by giving up the label fast path after exhaustion. The observed degradation and extra block storage leave its engineering case open. It is excluded from the candidate build.

The production candidate combines ancestor comparison and changed-range updates. Both share the existing compact stable-resident representation. Classical labeling can provide constant-work queries, so this comparison optimization does not establish parity or novelty. Snapshot and wire paths keep their existing ownership and sequencing. Cross-host equivalent-semantics measurements remain required before acceptance.
